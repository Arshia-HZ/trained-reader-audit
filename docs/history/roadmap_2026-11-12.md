Nov 12, 2026 · @Arshia

## Verdict

Keep one direction, but narrow its novelty claim. Fold the old Rank 2 into it as an ablation, and keep the old Rank 3 only as an optional thesis chapter. The three papers knock out two claims from the previous report:

- **That no aggregation paper uses no-candidate or placebo controls.** False: [2608.18379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18379) does, for an untrained, single-pass aggregator.
- **That resisting conformity needs training.** False: [2610.21290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21290) does it without training.

None of the three papers trains an aggregator, matches total compute against self-consistency (SC), or tests models of 1.7B or smaller. The novelty claim now sits there.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Train a small reader, and prove with controls that its gain over SC comes from reading the candidates (old 1, Stage 2) | 2608.18379 for the controls; 2610.21290 becomes a baseline | Keep, narrowed | About 40–55% for beating matched SC if the headroom gate passes. The attribution result is reportable either way. |
| 2 | Iterated aggregation (RSA) at 1.7B (old 1, Stage 1) | 2608.18379: aggregating over all-wrong pools hurts, so iterations may lock onto wrong answers | Inference-only baseline and secondary result, not the headline | Unknown at 1.7B |
| 3 | Distil the reader into one student (old 3) | 2608.18379 removes the all-wrong slice where it could have been new | Optional; runs only after gate G4 | Low; likely ties rejection-sampling SFT |
| — | Train the receiver to keep or switch (old 2) | 2610.21290 recovers 53.6% of vote failures and keeps 98.4% of vote successes without training; your audit shows revision itself costs about 14 points | Dropped as its own direction; becomes the minority-rescue training ablation in direction 1 | — |

I lowered the odds for direction 1 from the previous 50–60%. An untrained aggregator lost to plain voting when two or more candidates were right (0.887 vs 0.960, not significant), so a trained reader must first match the vote before it can beat it.

## How the three papers overlap with ours

The closest is 2608.18379. It runs the controls we planned for direction 1, but only on an untrained Qwen3-4B, in one pass, on 60 problems, and it names trained and iterated aggregators as open questions. All three are unreviewed arXiv preprints. Their numbers come from the HTML versions I read today.

What this means for each direction:

- **Direction 1:** high overlap in method, none in the trained setting. Adopt the 2608.18379 strata (c0/c1/c2+) and contrasts as the measurement standard and cite them as such. The contribution moves to what those controls show **after RL training**, at matched total tokens, at 0.6–1.7B, on 1,000+ problems.
- **Old Rank 2:** 2610.21290 already gets most of the conformity benefit without training, and your audit shows revision itself costs about 14 points. A trained keep-or-switch receiver would have to beat both. Testing that is cheap as an ablation and weak as a headline.
- **Distillation:** none of the papers targets it. At c0, though, untrained aggregation scored 0.155 against 0.277 for a fresh solve. Distilled aggregator outputs on all-wrong pools therefore carry nothing a fresh solve lacks, unless training changes that.
- **The Channel Audit:** 2608.18379 reproduces your placebo and copying results, which supports them but makes the form effect a known result. The audit stays useful as the revision-operator half of the comparison, measured under the same controls as the reader.

## Direction, gap and novelty claim

Train a small model to read a set of candidate solutions and give one answer (a reader). Then test, with controls and strict compute matching, whether its gain over SC comes from reading the candidates.

**Motivation from your audit.** Message content helped by about +14 to +17 points. The revision step that delivered it cost about 14, and at N = 3 only 2.6% of problems were recoverable. A reader replaces revision with a single read of a larger population. Untrained, full\_solution already rescued 37% of recoverable cases, so the open question is whether training makes reading beat cheap fresh samples.

| Question | Already done by | Still open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does a trained aggregator beat majority voting? | AggLM and SSA, at equal sample counts ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06870), [SSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09014)) | Matched total tokens at k ≤ 9; a 0.6–1.7B model reading its own samples |
| Where does aggregation gain come from (candidate-free, answer-only, placebo, c0/c1/c2+)? | 2608.18379: untrained, single pass, one 4B model, 60 problems | The same breakdown for a trained reader, before and after training, on 1,000+ problems |
| Can aggregation resist conformity? | 2610.21290 (training-free, mixed 8–14B teams); ACPO (DPO) | Small models from one family; whether RL lowers the copy rate beyond what deduplicated input does |
| Is the gain from aggregation or just from training? | Rarely checked; [2605.24202](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24202v2) matched training steps, not tokens | A solver trained with GRPO on the same generated training tokens, then run with SC |
| Does the split depend on the control? | 2610.18786, on RAG QA | Maths, with SC as the reference |

**Novelty claim:** the first compute-matched, counterfactually controlled test of whether an RL-trained small aggregator beats self-consistency *because it aggregates*. It separates selection, recombination and fresh solving, before and after training.

**No longer claimed:**

- placebo, candidate-free or answer-only controls as new;
- the form effect as new;
- a conformity fix as new.

Three targeted searches today found no paper that trains an aggregator and runs these controls. They were not exhaustive, and this area produced three related preprints in three months, so search again before each submission.

## Hypotheses and contributions

H1 is the positive result the thesis needs. H2–H4 make it defensible. Pre-register all thresholds before Stage 3, as you did for the audit.

1. **H1, positive effect.** A Qwen3-1.7B reader trained with GRPO, reading K = 4 candidates from base Qwen3-1.7B, beats base SC at matched generated tokens by at least 1.5 points on the primary test set (paired bootstrap 95% CI above 0). It also beats SC from a solver trained with GRPO on the same generated training tokens by at least 1.0 point.
2. **H2, it reads rather than re-solves.** After training, the reader's gain over its own candidate-free fresh solve grows at c1 by at least 10 points over the untrained reader. Its candidate-free accuracy rises by less than its full-input accuracy does. Pre-registered prediction from 2608.18379: the gain at c0 stays at or below 0.
3. **H3, the form effect shrinks.** With off-topic placebo candidates, the trained reader scores within 2 points of its own candidate-free accuracy (non-inferiority). The untrained baseline is −16.4 in 2608.18379 and about −14 in your audit.
4. **H4, conformity drops without losing the vote.** At c2+ the trained reader is non-inferior to plurality voting (margin 1 point). Its share of wrong outputs that copy a shown answer falls below the untrained reader's, and below the deduplicated-input baseline from 2610.21290.
5. **H5, exploratory.** Iterated aggregation (RSA, T > 1) beats matched SC at 1.7B on the primary set, and its per-step gain over a fresh solve tracks the share of c1 and c2+ sets in the population.

Expected contributions:

- **C1 (if H1 holds):** a compute-matched positive result for multi-agent reading at 0.6–1.7B, against both base SC and a matched single-agent RL baseline.
- **C2 (holds whatever the H1 outcome):** the first counterfactual breakdown of what RL training changes in an aggregator: selection, recombination, fresh solving and form. If the gain turns out to be mostly fresh solving, that is a reportable correction to the AggLM and SSA reading.
- **C3:** the bridge to your audit. Revision and reading are measured under the same controls and the same token budget, which explains why communication lost and when reading peers wins.
- **C4 (thesis only, optional):** a distillation chapter that keeps the proposal's second pillar.

| Reported, not powered | AIME 2025 + HMMT Feb 2025 | 60 | Direct comparison with 2608.18379 and RSA only |

Metrics, all computed per problem and then averaged:

- **Accuracy:** over 4 candidate-set draws × 2 replicates per problem, with paired problem-level bootstrap CIs and Holm correction over H1–H4.
- **Accuracy vs tokens:** two curves, generated tokens only (primary, as pre-registered in the audit) and generated plus reader prompt tokens.
- **Gain over a fresh solve:** reader with candidates minus reader without, by stratum c0/c1/c2+, as defined in 2608.18379.
- **Recovery and retention:** the share of vote failures fixed and of vote successes kept, as in 2610.21290.
- **Copy rate:** the share of wrong outputs that equal a shown answer.
- **Form effect:** placebo minus candidate-free accuracy.
- **Repetition gap:** Duplicate minus Single-slot accuracy, from 2610.18786.
- **Diversity and health:** pass@k, answer entropy, right-to-wrong flips, output length and truncation rate.

## Baselines and ablations

Reviewers can call the result "a better solver, not a better reader" unless two arms are in: the matched solver-GRPO baseline and the candidate-free control. Both are mandatory.

| Arm | Kind | What it rules out | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SC from base Qwen3-1.7B at matched generated tokens, and at matched generated + prompt tokens | Baseline | The primary comparison, under both token-accounting rules | Must |
| SC from a Qwen3-1.7B solver trained with GRPO on the same generated training tokens | Baseline | "Any RL training would have given this" | Must |
| Untrained reader, same prompt | Baseline | "Prompting alone does it"; also the bridge to your full\_solution arm | Must |
| Deduplicated input: one trace per distinct answer, in frequency order (2610.21290 bias control, without style normalisation because all candidates come from one model) | Baseline | "Hiding vote counts fixes conformity for free" | Must |
| RSA, T up to 5, N = 8, K ∈ {2, 4} | Baseline | "Iteration beats training" | Should |
| Votes weighted by Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B | Baseline | "A standard verifier does as well" | Should |
| Candidate-free: the reader solves from the problem alone, same output cap | Control | Fresh solving dressed up as aggregation | Must |
| Answer-only: shown answers without reasoning | Control | "Only the answer values matter" | Must |
| Placebo: length-matched solutions to other problems of the same subject and level | Control | The form effect of reading candidates | Must |
| Duplicate (one candidate × 4) and Single-slot (one candidate, 3 empty slots) | Control | Diversity vs repetition (2610.18786) | Should |

| Train on deduplicated input | Training ablation | Whether training adds anything beyond hiding vote counts | Should |

"Must" arms go in the paper's main table. "Should" arms go in ablations, and can be cut if Stage 3 runs late.

## Roadmap and go/no-go gates

The first training run starts only after about two weeks of cheap checks. Each check can redirect or stop the plan before GPU-weeks are spent.

&#91;embedded content: thesis roadmap · 5 stages, 4 gates\]

Stage 3 is the result the paper rests on. Every earlier gate exists to protect it from a null caused by small headroom or a broken harness.

1. **Stage 0, day 1: headroom on logs you have.** On the audit's confirm pools, compute by level:
   - pass@k − maj@k for k = 3, 5 and 9;
   - the share of problems where the right answer is in the minority;
   - the share where all samples agree on a wrong answer. This takes hours.
2. **Stage 0, days 2–3: new pools.** Generate 16 samples per problem for the dev, primary and secondary sets, then compute pass@8 − maj@8 on each. Gate G1 needs at least 5 points on the set you will call primary.
3. **Stage 1, days 4–5: harness check.** Run the untrained aggregation prompt with Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on AIME-25 + HMMT-25 at K = 4, with candidate-free, answer-only and placebo arms. Gate G2 needs the same signs as 2608.18379.
4. **Stage 1, days 6–10: untrained baselines on the primary set.** Run the untrained 1.7B and 4B readers with every control, plus deduplicated input, the PRM-weighted vote, and an RSA grid (N = 8, K ∈ {2, 4}, T ≤ 5). This table is the "before training" column of C2 and answers H5.
5. **Stage 2, days 11–14: training pools and pilot.** Build the 8k-problem train pools and candidate sets. Run a 50-step GRPO pilot and measure throughput. Gate G3.
6. **Stage 3, weeks 3–5: main runs.** First freeze H1–H4 and the checkpoint rule. Then run the reader with 3 seeds and solver-GRPO with 3 seeds, the solver stopping at matched tokens. Then run the no-c0, deduplicated-input, minority-rescue and 0.6B ablations with 1 seed each.
7. **Stage 3, week 6: evaluation.** Run every arm and control on the primary and secondary sets, with 4 candidate-set draws × 2 replicates, then the frozen analysis. Gate G4 decides how the paper is framed.
8. **Stage 4, weeks 7–8 (optional): distillation.** Only if G4 passes. Fine-tune one 1.7B student on reader outputs, and another on an equal number of rejection-sampled correct solutions, then compare them at matched inference tokens.
9. **Transfer and write-up.** Run one evaluation pass in which the trained reader reads Llama-3.2-3B candidates. Then write the paper, with the audit as its diagnostic half.

## Implementation details

Reuse the audit harness as it is: grader with `math_verify`, deterministic seeds, append-only store, analysis freeze. The reader is ordinary single-turn GRPO, where a prompt holds the problem plus its candidates. No multi-agent RL framework is needed.

**Candidate pools.** Draw 16 samples per problem from base Qwen3-1.7B at temperature 0.7 (as in the audit), for the train, dev and test splits. Estimate SC at any k by subsampling these pools, so every SC point is free once the pools exist. Cap each candidate at 1,536 tokens inside the reader prompt and keep the full solution. 2608.18379 cut candidates at their first answer, so state the difference.

**Training sets.** For each train problem, draw candidate sets of K = 4 from its pool and label each set c0, c1 or c2+. Following AggLM, keep every hard set (majority answer wrong) and add an equal number of easy sets ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06870)). Shuffle candidate order. Never label which candidate is the reader's own sample.

**GRPO starting values.** Tune these in the pilot; they are not tested:

- full fine-tune in BF16 (1.7B fits on 96 GB with room for vLLM rollouts);
- learning rate 1e-6 with 10 warm-up steps;
- 64 prompts per step, group size 8, rollout temperature 1.0;
- prompt cap 7k tokens, response cap 2k;
- PPO clip 0.2 low and 0.28 high, KL coefficient 0 or 0.001;
- 300 steps, checkpoint every 25.

**Reward.** 1 if the boxed answer is correct under `math_verify`, otherwise 0; a missing box counts as wrong. Fix the checkpoint rule before Stage 3: the last checkpoint, or the best of steps 100/200/300 on dev, never the peak of a continuous curve.

**Solver-GRPO baseline.** The same recipe on the same problems, with no candidates in the prompt. Stop it when its cumulative generated rollout tokens equal the reader run's.

**Token accounting.** The reader's budget per problem is the K candidates' generated tokens plus the reader's own output (generated-only rule):

```latex
B_{\text{read}} = \sum_{i=1}^{K} g_i + g_{\text{read}}, \qquad k^{*} = B_{\text{read}} / \bar{g}_{\text{solve}}
```

SC accuracy at fractional k\* is linearly interpolated between neighbouring integer k, as in the audit. The secondary rule adds the reader's prompt tokens to B. Count each problem's own tokens; do not use averages across problems.

**Compute on the RTX PRO 6000.** Every figure below is my unmeasured estimate. Measure throughput in a 20-step pilot before committing.

| Job | Size | Rough time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test and dev pools | About 2.5k problems × 16 samples × 0.8k tokens ≈ 32M generated tokens | Hours |
| Train pools | 8k × 16 × 0.8k ≈ 100M generated tokens | Under a day |
| One GRPO run, 1.7B, 300 steps | 300 × 512 rollouts × up to 2k tokens ≈ 150–300M generated tokens, plus 7k-token prompts | 15–30 h |
| All runs: 3 reader seeds, 3 solver seeds, about 4 ablations | About 10 runs | 1.5–3 GPU-weeks |
| Evaluation of every arm and control | About 100–200k generations | 1–2 days |

**Software.**

- Stay in BF16 and avoid FP8: block-scaled FP8 crashes at load on this GPU family under vLLM unless `VLLM_USE_DEEP_GEMM=0` is set ([vLLM #47436](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/47436)).
- Pin one torch, CUDA and vLLM combination, and fall back to SDPA attention if FlashAttention wheels fail.
- Run rollouts on the same GPU as training (colocated).

## Risks, limitations and fallbacks

The biggest risk is that training makes a better solver rather than a better reader. The plan does not try to avoid that outcome; it measures it, so the paper survives either answer.

| Risk | Early signal | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too little headroom: pass@8 − maj@8 under 5 points | Stage 0 numbers | Use OlympiadBench as the primary set. If it is still under 5, run Stage 1 only and write the controlled study of untrained and iterated aggregation. |
| Gain is fresh solving: candidate-free accuracy rises as much as full-input accuracy | The no-c0 ablation keeps the gain only on c1/c2+ | Report C2 as the headline ("trained aggregators mostly learn to re-solve") and frame it as a correction to AggLM and SSA. Aim for Findings or TMLR. |
| Solver-GRPO matches the reader | Seed-averaged H1 second clause | Same as above. H1 fails honestly. Do not drop the baseline to rescue H1. |
| Training collapses diversity, or the reader copies the majority | Copy rate and answer entropy on dev every 25 steps | Switch to deduplicated-input training, add KL 0.001, or lower the learning rate |
| Scooped: three related preprints appeared from August to October 2026 | A monthly search for "trained aggregator" with a candidate-free or placebo control | Post a preprint as soon as Stage 3 numbers are final |
| Reviewers attack the token accounting | Both curves exist by design | Lead with the prompt-inclusive curve if the gap holds there |
| Forking paths from wanting a positive result | Any change to H1–H4 after Stage 3 starts | Freeze hypotheses, thresholds and the checkpoint rule first; label everything else exploratory |
| The software stack breaks on Blackwell | Load or kernel errors in the pilot | BF16, pinned versions, SDPA attention |

Limits to state in the paper:

- one model family for candidates in the main result;
- maths only;
- non-thinking mode only;
- 2608.18379 and 2610.21290 are unreviewed preprints, so their numbers may change.

If both H1 and C2 come out weak, the thesis falls back to two diagnostic chapters on one shared harness (the Channel Audit and the untrained-reader study), plus the matched solver-GRPO comparison.
