# Prior work took the reader, not its attribution

The roadmap's novelty claim is not open as worded. A GRPO-trained Qwen3-1.7B aggregator that reads Qwen3-1.7B non-thinking samples already beats maj@8 by **+4.4 to +11.9 points** ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)), and a DAPO-trained Qwen3-1.7B selector does the same job ([RL-GenSelect](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02143v1)). On 1 November 2026, TTT-Consolidate (NeurIPS 2026) compared a trained Qwen3-1.7B/4B consolidator with majority voting at matched FLOPs and by pool tier. It also ran candidate-free, repetition and other-problem controls, plus an answer-swap test in which **RL-trained in-context aggregators lost up to 64 points** ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)). What survives is narrower. Nobody has yet tested an in-context RL reader of its own model's samples with the full 2608.18379 battery (candidate-free fresh solve, answer-only and placebo, split by c0/c1/c2+), before and after training, at ≤1.7B and on 1,000+ problems, against per-problem token-matched self-consistency (SC) and a solver-GRPO baseline matched on training tokens. That gap is defensible but incremental, and the audit's own SC curve makes a positive result risky. At K = 4 the reader must beat SC at k* ≈ 5 (**73.4%**) when only generated tokens count, and at k* ≈ 8 (**about 76%**) once its prompt counts, yet only **2.6%** of the audit's replicates had a right answer outvoted ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). The strongest upgrade within 2–4 GPU-weeks moves part of the thesis to the solver side. Its core is a factorial of solver objective (GRPO, pass@k, or credit for helping a frozen trained reader) × aggregator (vote, untrained reader, trained reader), joined by a joint solver-and-reader arm, a vote-or-read gate tested against adaptive SC, and answer-swap controls. Every joint-training paper so far uses one shared model at 4B or larger, and none compares against token-matched SC or splits results by stratum. The roadmap must also add zero-cost log-probability selectors and adaptive SC; three compute axes, with 32 samples per test problem so that prompt-inclusive matching can actually be estimated (the audit's could not); a contamination probe with perturbed and post-release test sets; and a power-based H1, whose 1.0-point solver clause has only about **40–55% power** on the planned primary set.

## Five papers already occupy the roadmap's "still open" column

The roadmap lists two open items for trained aggregators, "matched total tokens at k ≤ 9" and "a 0.6–1.7B model reading its own samples" ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md)), and the second is gone. **AggLM-1.7B** starts from Qwen3-1.7B. It is trained with GRPO on a binary math_verify reward over about 40k DeepScaleR problems, with 128 Qwen3-1.7B solutions each. Evaluated at K = 8 on Qwen3-1.7B non-thinking candidates, which is exactly the roadmap's setting, it beats maj@8 by 11.9, 4.4, 8.3 and 5.5 points on AIME24, AIME25, HMMT24 and HMMT25 ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)). The same paper makes three further claims. Aggregating 8 solutions beats voting over 16. Fine-tuning the solver instead gives small or negative gains, though that control matches data, not tokens. And a single model trained both to solve and to aggregate lands within 0.4–1.8 points of separate models ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)). RL-trained GenSelect puts a DAPO-trained Qwen3-1.7B selector over Qwen3-1.7B candidates and lifts HMMT25 from 25.00 (maj@8) to 36.25. It excludes all-wrong pools from training by design and never matches compute against SC ([RL-GenSelect](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02143v1)). SSA trains 0.5B–3B aggregators with GRPO and finds that RL collapses their outputs to a template plus an answer, so in practice they select rather than synthesise ([SSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09014v2)).

**The closest scoop appeared on 1 November 2026, eleven days before the roadmap was written, and the roadmap's searches missed it.** TTT-Consolidate (arXiv 2611.02104, NeurIPS 2026) freezes Qwen3-1.7B or Qwen3-4B and fits a LoRA adapter on the parallel trajectories at test time. It meta-learns the adapter's starting point with SFT or GRPO-style RLVR ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)). It compares against majority voting (MV) at matched FLOPs: on Minority-tier pools, MV@8/13/18 scores 22.7/22.2/22.6 against 35.4 for TTT@8. It splits every result into Minority, Majority and None tiers, which correspond to c1, c2+ and c0. Its controls map onto the roadmap's ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)):

| TTT-Consolidate control | Accuracy |
| --- | --- |
| No trajectories | 7.5% |
| Question repeated eight times | 5.4% |
| Trajectories from other problems | 4.0% |
| Single-Trace×8, then vote | 11.7% |
| Full method | 22.0% |

The tier for those control numbers was not confirmed. The result most damaging to the roadmap's C2 is an answer-swap test on Majority-tier pools. **Prompted in-context aggregators lose up to 52 points and RL-trained ones up to 64, against 5–17 for TTT.** The authors read this as evidence that in-context aggregators vote rather than read. Their in-context baselines also recover only 0.8–6.7% of all-wrong pools ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)). The roadmap's own setting stays uncovered in four ways. TTT-Consolidate's trajectories come from six external LLMs, not from the reader's own model. Its method changes weights rather than reading in context. Its reference is MV, not SC matched on generated tokens. And it has no candidate-free fresh-solve contrast within each stratum.

The question "is the gain just a better solver?" has also been asked, but only at 4B and above, and the answers lean against a separate reader. Standard RL makes Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 a *worse* input to recursive aggregation than the untrained reference in 4 of 5 tasks, while aggregation-aware RL always beats standard RL ([RSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26626v2)). On Qwen3-4B-Base, three prompted aggregation rounds lower the competition-maths average from 15.45 to 11.54. After training, Dr.GRPO reaches 28.13, an offline AggLM-style aggregator 31.46, naive online multitask 27.46, and ParaGator, which gives candidates a pass@k advantage, 34.00 ([ParaGator](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18886v1)). SPIRAL trains one 4B model to produce trace sets that are useful to its own aggregator, against a GRPO baseline matched at 98,304 training tokens per problem. It finds that **GRPO and SPIRAL perform similarly under majority voting**, and SPIRAL pulls ahead only under recursive aggregation ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23595v2)). At Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B, single-agent RL matches or beats trained Voting workflows (1.7B: 50.5 vs 49.9). That study matches steps rather than tokens, has no SC baseline and runs one seed per cell ([2605.24202](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24202v2)). At equal token cost, no untrained method reliably beats repeated sampling from 1.5B to 7B; at 1.5B, self-selection of the best of 8 loses to MV by 8.0 and 11.3 points ([2607.28576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28576)).

Four citation details in the roadmap are now out of date. First, 2608.18379 has a **v2 (19 October 2026)** and was **accepted at the COLM 2026 Workshop on Efficient Reasoning**, so "all three are unreviewed preprints" is wrong ([2608.18379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18379)). Second, its placebo arm ran **only on c0 problems**. There, aggregation minus placebo is +0.045 [−0.018, +0.118]: aggregating all-wrong pools cannot be told apart from reading off-topic solutions ([2608.18379 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18379v2)). Third, its answer-only gap at c2+ is +0.161 under first-answer scoring but +0.008 under final-answer scoring, and its c1 contrast is unresolved (p = 0.0755). Gate G2's "same signs as 2608.18379" should therefore be judged only at c0 and c2+ ([2608.18379 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18379v2)). Fourth, 2610.21290 does budget-match its combined-MV baseline "at or above" its own token use, which is 9–12% lower ([2610.21290](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21290v1)). The roadmap's sentence that none of the three papers matches total compute needs qualifying.

2608.18379 itself names the opening that remains: "We have not tested a learned selector" ([2608.18379 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18379v2)). Semantic Scholar listed no papers citing it as of 13 November ([S2](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2608.18379/citations?fields=title,externalIds,publicationDate,venue&limit=100)). Even so, the risk of being scooped is high: AggLM's FAIR authors have already moved on to joint training (ParaGator), the EPFL group behind TTT-Consolidate has already run placebo-style controls, and related preprints arrived roughly monthly through 2026. The search behind this report relied on the citation graphs of AggLM, RSA and SSA (51, 28 and 35 citing papers) plus keyword searches. It did not browse the NeurIPS 2026 or EMNLP 2026 accepted-paper lists, or ICLR 2027 OpenReview.

## The audit's own SC curve sets a two-to-five-point break-even

Gate G1 asks for pass@8 − maj@8 ≥ 5 points. That measures headroom but ignores what the reader costs in SC samples, and the audit's own curve sets that price. On the 600 dev problems, SC scores **63.7% at k = 1, 69.7% at k = 3, 73.4% at k = 5 and 76.8% at k = 9**, and one sample costs about 819 generated tokens ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)).

Under the pre-registered generated-only rule, a K = 4 reader pays for four candidates plus its own output, about one sample if it writes a solution, so k* ≈ 5. Under the prompt-inclusive rule, it also pays to re-read about 3.3k candidate tokens plus the problem. Charging roughly 200 problem tokens to every SC sample as well gives k* ≈ 8. For a reader the same size as the solver, this second rule is the same as FLOP matching, because a dense model spends about 2·N_params FLOPs per token in prefill and in decode.

A four-sample vote lies between 69.7% and 73.4%. So the reader must gain about 2 points over its own vote just to tie SC under the primary rule, and about 5 points under the prompt-inclusive rule. H1's +1.5 margin then comes on top. The table interpolates the audit's curve at about 0.85 points per sample between k = 5 and k = 9; the interpolation and the two lower rows are my calculations.

| Accounting rule, K = 4 | Reader cost in SC samples | k* | SC at k* (dev, L3–5) | Reader accuracy H1 requires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generated tokens only (pre-registered primary) | 4 + ≈1 | ≈5 | 73.4% | ≥74.9% |
| Generated + prompt tokens (= FLOPs for a same-size reader) | 4 + ≈4.1 | ≈8 | ≈76.0% | ≥77.5% |
| FLOPs, with a 0.6B reader (prompt and output at 0.35× weight) | 4 + ≈1.5 | ≈5.5 | ≈73.8% | ≥75.3% |
| Generated + prompt, with a unanimity gate that reads 45% of problems (illustrative) | 4 + ≈1.9 | ≈5.9 | ≈74.1% | ≥75.6% |

The headroom available to pay that price is thin. With three samples, only **31 of 1,200 replicates (2.6%)** had a right answer that the vote missed. Untrained `full_solution` reading recovered **36.6% [20.4, 53.8]** of them, about 11 cases, but broke about 22 of the 801 replicates the vote already had right ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). Even AggLM's trained reader recovered only 27–57% of the pass@8 − maj@8 gap on 1.7B thinking candidates (my calculation from [AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)'s tables).

The reader's gain over its own vote decomposes as:

**gain = P(minority-correct) · rescue rate − P(majority-correct) · break rate + P(c0) · fresh-solve rate**

The 18 independent samples per dev problem give every share in that formula exactly, with no new generation. Enumerating all 3,060 four-sample subsets of 18 per problem yields the c0/c1/c2+ mix, the minority-correct share, the unanimity rate, and SC up to k = 18.

That supports a sharper Stage 0 gate. Compute the break-even, SC(k*) − maj@K, for each accounting rule and for K ∈ {4, 8}; multiply the available headroom by a plausible recovery rate such as AggLM's 27–57%; and make H1 the headline only if that product clears the break-even plus 1.5 points. Otherwise, lead with C2 and the factorial from the start. The audit also favours level 5 as the primary set. Round-0 disagreement was 53% at level 5 against 29% at level 3. `full_solution`'s gap to SC was −0.5 [−3.7, +2.8] at level 5, against −2.9 at level 3 ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)).

Because the break-even depends on both K and the accounting rule, **K is a first-order design variable, not an ablation**. Under generated-only accounting, the reader's overhead stays near one sample whatever K is, while SC's slope falls from about 1.85 points per sample (k = 3 to 5) to about 0.85 (k = 5 to 9). So the price of reading shrinks as K grows, and the headroom grows with it. That argues for K = 8, the value AggLM trained and tested at. Under prompt-inclusive accounting, reading K candidates costs about as much as generating them, so k* ≈ 2K + 1, and on a roughly log-shaped SC curve that price does not shrink.

Longer contexts also hurt. 2608.18379's AIME c0 accuracy fell to 0.037 at K = 8 ([2608.18379 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18379v2)), RSA's gains flatten beyond K = 3 ([RSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26626v2)), and failed attempts in context cost 10–20% across 11 models ([Contextual Drag](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04288)). RL-GenSelect already trains across pool sizes from 2 to 16 ([RL-GenSelect](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02143v1)), so training on mixed K ∈ {2, 4, 8} is cheap and makes a pre-registered K sweep interpretable.

Two cheap levers cut the price of reading with little risk to accuracy. The first is a gate that runs the reader only when the K answers disagree. In the audit, 61.4% of three-agent round-0 teams already had at most one distinct answer ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). On agreeing sets reading adds little: AggLM is "on par" with MV when the majority is large ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)), untrained aggregation lost to the vote at c2+, 0.960 vs 0.887 ([2608.18379 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18379v2)), and in-context aggregators recover under 7% of all-wrong pools ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)). The second lever is a 0.6B reader. Under FLOP accounting it pays about 35% as much per token as a 1.7B reader, and SSA-0.5B already outscored a 7B PRM (54.71 vs 52.97 on average) ([SSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09014v2)). A third effect points the same way by accident: a reader whose RL outputs collapse to a short selection, as SSA's did, costs almost nothing to run. But such a reader can no longer support any claim about recombination.

My judgement follows from these numbers. H1's first clause, under generated-only accounting on level 5, is roughly a coin flip. The prompt-inclusive version is unlikely without the gate or the 0.6B reader. The solver-GRPO clause is the weakest, because SPIRAL and 2605.24202 both found that standard RL closes most of the gap under voting.

## Solver-side training is the open lane at 1.7B

Every strong 2026 design trains the solver as well as, or instead of, a separate reader. RSA uses aggregation-aware RL ([RSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26626v2)), ParaGator gives candidates a pass@k advantage ([ParaGator](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18886v1)), SPIRAL credits each trace by its marginal value to the sets that contain it ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23595v2)), and GSR finds that a hybrid solve-plus-refine objective beats either alone, AIME24 51.5 vs 45.0 vs 44.3 ([GSR](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00084v2)). The shared motivation is diversity collapse. Standard outcome RL "collapses candidate generation toward one dominant mode" ([ParaGator blog](https://facebookresearch.github.io/RAM/blogs/paragator/)). Olmo 3 7B's SFT-distilled Think line gains only +0.4% from majority voting on GSM8K, against +22–26% for RL-Zero, so the collapse depends on training data, not on RL alone ([2604.16027](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16027)). And with group-dependent rewards, naive GRPO "collapses onto the majority answer" ([Team-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02404)).

**Every joint-training result so far uses one shared model at 4B or larger.** None uses ≤1.7B non-thinking models, a frozen separate reader, token-matched base SC, or stratified attribution controls. SPIRAL's Appendix B describes a two-model variant, but no experiments with it were visible ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23595v2)). That is the open lane, and it suits the student's measurement discipline. The five ideas below are ranked by evidence per GPU-hour.

**The first idea costs nothing beyond evaluation.** The reader and solver-GRPO are trained anyway, so the grid {base, solver-GRPO candidates} × {vote, untrained reader, trained reader} is free. It tests at 1.7B two findings that so far exist only at 4B: RSA's result that standard RL produces worse inputs for aggregation, and SPIRAL's result that GRPO and set RL tie under voting.

**The second idea is a joint solver-and-reader arm.** One model is trained with GRPO on a 50/50 mix of plain and reading prompts, as in RSA's aggregation-aware RL and AggLM's multitask variant. It costs about one extra run, and its own weights give the cleanest attribution available: reading with candidates minus the candidate-free solve, on the same parameters. It should read offline base-model candidates. At 4B, naive *online* multitask (27.46) trailed both the offline AggLM-style aggregator (31.46) and Dr.GRPO (28.13) ([ParaGator](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18886v1)).

**The third idea, a solver trained against a frozen trained reader, is the high-upside chapter.** It crosses four solver objectives {base, pass@1 GRPO, pass@k advantage, reader-marginal credit} with three aggregators {vote, untrained reader, frozen trained reader}. Pass@k objectives raise large-k coverage but can cost pass@1. Tang et al. report +12 points at p@100 and −5.6 at p@1 ([Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19595v2)), and Chen et al. raise AIME25 pass@k from 19.1 to 22.4 at 7B ([Pass@k Training](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10751v1)). Reader-marginal credit rewards each solver trace by the frozen reader's correctness on sampled sets that contain it. SPIRAL's set-average estimator needs no extra reader calls. The leave-one-out estimator, which Team-GRPO proves unbiased, is the fallback if a pilot shows high variance ([Team-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02404)).

The pre-registered predictions follow SPIRAL and the Olmo result. GRPO should raise maj@K but lower answer entropy and shrink the reader's margin over the vote. Reader-marginal training should raise the c1 share and the frozen reader's accuracy without raising maj@K. Together, those results would separate diversity that helps a vote from diversity that helps a reader, which nobody has measured with c0/c1/c2+ strata.

Freezing the reader has two further benefits. It separates "the solver learned to feed the reader" from "the reader learned to read". It also makes collusion measurable, through the frozen reader's copy rate on the new solver's wrong candidates; the audit saw 33.4% wrong-copying under `full_solution` ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). The risks are documented. The naive k-sample gradient has O(k) variance ([Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19595v2)), an entropy bonus of 0.005 collapsed training ([Pass@k Training](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10751v1)), and SPIRAL has already shown the end-to-end version at 4B. So the chapter must be framed as decomposition at small scale with a frozen reader, not as a new method.

**The fourth idea, a vote-or-read gate, is mainly an accounting contribution.** It comes with a sharp question: given that the samples disagree, is one read worth more than more votes? It needs a fair counterpart. Early-stopping and adaptive SC use the same agreement signal to stop sampling ([ESC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10480), [Adaptive-Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11860)), so a gated reader must beat adaptive SC, not plain SC.

The evidence cuts both ways. PPV's "do-no-harm" result says delegation should not overturn near-unanimous majorities ([PPV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08098)). But in matched-budget debate at 8B, 66% of the examples that debate helped were unanimous-but-wrong votes, a vote-entropy threshold gained only +1.6 pp (not significant), and learned controllers did not beat it ([2605.09618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09618)). For a reader, a unanimous-but-wrong set offers only a fresh solve, which in-context aggregators manage under 7% of the time; that bounds what the gate gives up. A fixed unanimity rule should be the pre-registered gate. A learned gate stays exploratory, whether it is a trained verification bit or an ODSA-style orchestrator that chooses between solving, comparing and accepting ([ODSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11621)).

**The fifth idea is a set of controls that separate voting from reading.** The first is an answer swap. In c2+ and c1 sets, replace the majority cluster's final answer with a wrong value everywhere it appears in its last step, leaving the reasoning intact. A voter follows the swapped answer; a reader recovers the derived one. TTT-Consolidate's result predicts that RL *increases* reliance on answer frequency, which gives H4 a falsifiable partner ([TTT-Consolidate](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02104v1)).

Placebo should run on every stratum, since 2608.18379 measured it only on c0 problems. Position and length bias should also be reported: accuracy by the slot of the lone correct candidate in c1 sets, and selection against candidate length. No trained-aggregator maths paper reports either; 2608.18379 explicitly skipped position bias, and SPIRAL flags ordering bias ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23595v2)). Theory backs the answer-only control as well: no procedure that reads only the answer distribution can always tell majority-right from majority-wrong questions ([Agreement Is Not Truth, EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.631/)).

**Learned channels, critics, latent links, heterogeneous teams and distillation are the weakest uses of the remaining budget.** The one learned-message paper with an SC baseline, Optima, still lost to SC on MATH at 8B (30.4 vs 35.7), at about a tenth of the tokens ([Optima](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08115v2)). RL-trained critics do work, with Critique-RL lifting a 3B actor on MATH from 36.9 to 48.6, but the smallest trained critic-refiner found is 3B, it was trained on 8× A800, and it was compared with voting by sample count ([Critique-RL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24320v1)). The audit adds a structural handicap: any channel that feeds N revisers pays for N regenerations, about 2.2 samples per revision round, while a reader pays for one output ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). Latent channels leave the 0.6–1.7B maths cell empty, since C2C's 0.6B receiver is tested only on multiple-choice knowledge tasks and LatentMAS starts at 4B, but no surveyed latent method is RL-trained or compared at matched compute ([latent survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05711v3), [C2C](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03215v1), [LatentMAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20639v2)). Heterogeneous LoRA teams are risky at exactly this scale, where same-role parallel adapters suffer gradient amplification and degrade late in training ([2605.24202](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24202v2)). Distillation also stays out: Phase 1 found full transcripts at 49.8% against 48.9% for rejection-sampled solutions ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)), and AggLM lists distilling the aggregator into the solver only as future work ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)). The one channel idea worth keeping is a trained verification bit that feeds the gate, because the audit's best channel was a one-bit dissent signal (−0.8 [−2.0, +0.3]). It stays exploratory.

The budget below is my extrapolation from the roadmap's unmeasured estimate of 15–30 hours per 1.7B GRPO run, with about 1.5–2× that for reader-marginal solver steps. verl documents single-GPU GRPO for Qwen3-1.7B on 80 GB, so a frozen 1.7B reader can share the 96 GB card ([Qwen docs: verl](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/stable/training/verl.html)).

| Job | Runs | Estimated GPU-hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test pools at 32 samples; train pools at 16 | — | ≈24 |
| Reader GRPO, mixed K | 3 seeds | 45–90 |
| Solver GRPO, matched on rollout + pool tokens (step-300 checkpoint kept as the equal-steps variant) | 3 seeds | 60–150 |
| Joint solver-and-reader | 2 seeds | 35–70 |
| Solver trained against the frozen reader | 2 seeds | 50–120 |
| Pass@k-advantage solver | 1 seed | 15–30 |
| Reader trained without c0 sets | 1 seed | 15–30 |
| 0.6B reader | 1 seed | 8–15 |
| Evaluation of all arms, controls, the 2×2 and the K sweep | — | 48–96 |
| **Total** | | **≈300–625 h, or ≈1.8–3.7 GPU-weeks** |

The upper end fits only after the cuts in the ranked list: distillation, two training ablations and the long RSA grid. The frozen-reader solver should run last, so that it is the first thing cut.

## Reviewers will attack accounting and baselines before results

The roadmap has no baseline that costs zero extra generation, and those are the first a reviewer will ask for. DeepConf (ICLR 2026) filters and weights votes by local token confidence and reports up to 84.7% fewer tokens, but on 8B+ reasoning models; an independent replication on web agents found only +0.6 points ([DeepConf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15260), [replication](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12276)). CISC beats SC with over 40% fewer paths across 9 models ([CISC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06233v2)). Self-certainty Borda voting ([self-certainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18581v3)) and shortest-answer selection ([short-m@k](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17813v2)) are also free. A verbalized-confidence vote costs nothing at all, because every stored audit sample already states `Confidence: N` ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)).

No study runs these selectors on ≤2B non-thinking models under strict token matching. They will likely tie SC, and a tie is useful: it rules out "the reader just learned confidence weighting". If the dev samples were stored without log-probabilities, one prefill pass recovers them.

Verifier and judge baselines must pay for their compute. Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B has about 4× Qwen3-1.7B's parameters, so scoring K = 4 solutions costs roughly four times the FLOPs of generating them. Under FLOP matching, PRM-vote@4 should therefore face SC at about k = 20. Generative verification needs up to 8× more compute to match SC, and wins only at high budgets ([Singhi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01005v2)). AggLM-1.7B itself belongs in the main table if its checkpoint is public (not verified), run through the student's own harness. Copied baseline numbers are how "incorrect baseline" disputes start ([Chandak et al.](https://safe-lip-9a8.notion.site/Incorrect-Baseline-Evaluations-Call-into-Question-Recent-LLM-RL-Claims-2012f1fbf0ee8094ab8ded1953c15a37)).

| Arm | What it rules out | Extra cost | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DeepConf-offline and self-certainty Borda vote | "The reader learned confidence weighting" | None (log-probabilities) | Must |
| Vote weighted by the stored `Confidence: N` | The same, with the audit's own signal | None | Must |
| Early-stopping or adaptive SC | "The gate is just adaptive compute" | Saves samples | Must if the reader is gated |
| Shortest-answer selection | Length heuristics | None | Should |
| CISC with P(True); USC (universal self-consistency: the model picks the most consistent answer) | Prompted self-weighting and selection | One prefill per sample, or one reader pass, counted | Should |
| PRM-weighted vote, with verifier FLOPs counted | "A standard verifier does as well" | ≈4× generation FLOPs | Should |
| AggLM-1.7B checkpoint, if released | A faulty reimplementation | One reader pass | Must if public |
| RSA with N = 8, K = 4, T ≤ 3, generated-only accounting | "Iteration beats training" | Up to 32 generations | Should |
| Pairwise knockout judged by base Qwen3-1.7B | "Pairwise comparison suffices" ([knockout](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19477v5)) | Judge passes, counted | Optional |

The accounting needs three axes, because each rule rewards something different. Generated-only accounting, the audit's pre-registered rule, treats reading as free. Generated-plus-prompt accounting equals FLOP matching for a same-size reader, and it should weight tokens by parameter count for the 0.6B reader and the PRM. Measured GPU-seconds in vLLM, with prefix caching on for SC, form the third axis: Kinetics (NeurIPS 2025) shows that FLOP-only accounting overrates small models, because KV-cache memory traffic and attention dominate long generations, and prefill is much cheaper per token than decode ([Kinetics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05333v2)). Wall-clock time therefore falls between the other two rules, and it is the most defensible tie-breaker.

The audit's own sensitivity run is a warning. When prompt tokens were counted, most arms' budgets went past the nine drawn samples, and their contrasts were "not estimable" ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). At K = 8 the prompt-inclusive k* reaches about 17, and PRM voting needs about 20. RSA at N = 8 needs 8(T + 1) generations even under the generated-only rule. **The planned 16 samples per test problem are too few.** About 32 are needed, roughly 51M generated tokens for 2,000 problems. On the roadmap's own estimate (32M tokens take "hours"), that adds a few hours. Even then, RSA should be reported only under generated-only accounting and only up to T = 3.

The solver-GRPO match needs the same care. The roadmap stops the solver when its rollout tokens equal the reader's. That leaves out two costs the reader pipeline carries: about 100M tokens of training pools, by the roadmap's own estimate, and the 7k-token prefill behind every reader rollout ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md)). SPIRAL matched total per-problem training tokens ([SPIRAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23595v2)). The solver should therefore be matched on rollout plus pool tokens, with its step-300 checkpoint saved as the equal-steps variant, and the stronger of the two used as the H1 comparator. Reviewers will pick the strongest version anyway.

## The test sets need a contamination probe and a power budget

The primary set must exclude every problem the audit has already looked at, not only the confirm set. MATH level 5 has **1,324** test problems ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/math-level-5)), and 208 of the 600 dev problems are level 5. That leaves at most 1,116, before removing the level-5 problems in the screen and pilot splits. Those splits informed design choices such as the prompts and the `dissent_only` pick ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). The confirm problems beyond the first 600 were never generated on, and they form a ready power reserve.

Contamination is a live threat. Qwen3 base models can reconstruct MATH-500 problems from partial prompts: from a 60% prefix, Qwen3-4B-Base gets 21.8% exact match and Qwen3-14B-Base 43.6%, against 5.2% for post-trained Qwen3-4B ([2507.10532](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10532v3)). High sampling temperature reduces the effect ([2601.04301](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04301v1)), but no probe of Qwen3-1.7B has been published. The probe is cheap: greedy decoding from a 60% prefix, scored by exact match, on Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-1.7B, over the level-5 and OlympiadBench problems. H2 should then be reported split by probe outcome, because memorised answers can pass for fresh solving.

Three further test sets close the remaining gaps. **MATH-P-Hard** is the robustness set: 279 level-5 problems perturbed so that the original solution path fails, on which all 18 tested models dropped 10–25% ([MATH-Perturb](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06453v2)). Its seed problems include MATH *train* items, so either drop the items seeded from train problems or remove those seeds from the training pools. **A post-release pool** of HMMT Nov 2025, AIME 2026 and HMMT Feb 2026 gives 30 + 30 + 33 = **93 problems** ([MathArena](https://matharena.ai/competitions)). It should be reported with Bayesian intervals ([Don't Pass@k](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04265v2)) rather than as a powered test, since 1.7B non-thinking maj@8 sits at 7–18% on the 2024/25 competitions ([AggLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06870)). AIME25 and HMMT Feb 25 stay, but only for comparison with 2608.18379, given MathArena's strong signs of contamination in AIME 2024 ([MathArena paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23281)).

Two harness details from the audit also matter. First, with the round-0 prompt, Llama-3.2-3B left 70.4% of outputs unboxed, and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B left 18.1% unboxed and 28.7% truncated. A plain prompt brought Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct back to 52.0% with 0.3% unboxed ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). The roadmap's transfer pools therefore need a plain prompt and a format gate. Second, the dev pools were generated in fp16 on vLLM 0.33.0, while the roadmap plans BF16. Every candidate pool should come from one pinned stack, since the audit already measured vLLM outputs changing with batch composition.

The power arithmetic exposes H1's thresholds. The audit's pilot measured paired SDs of **0.16–0.20** per problem, which implied 814 problems for a 2-point effect ([audit](https://github.com/Arshia-HZ/mas-channel-audit/blob/audit-final/reports/channel_audit_M0-M7_report.md)). With n ≈ 1,100 and SD 0.18, my calculation gives a 95% half-width of about 1.1 points and a minimum detectable effect of about 1.5 points at 80% power. The 1.0-point solver clause has only about **40–55% power**, before seed variance is even added. As worded, H1 also requires a point estimate of at least 1.5 points together with CI > 0. That test fails half the time even when the true effect is exactly 1.5. The fix is to restate the first clause as superiority (CI lower bound > 0) with 1.5 as the planning effect, to pool the solver clause across the primary and secondary sets or pre-register it as an estimate with a non-inferiority bound, and to measure the reader's paired SD on dev in Stage 1 before fixing the test-set size for Stage 3.

The rest of the statistical protection is standard but must be written down. At least three seeds should run for both the reader and solver-GRPO, compared seed mean against seed mean with a permutation test over seeds and with every seed reported, since on a 30-problem set one question moves pass@1 by more than 3 points ([Sober Look](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07086v2)). The bootstrap should be nested and paired, resampling problems and then candidate-set draws and replicates within each problem. H1–H4 should form one Holm family, and the factorial and gate hypotheses a second. Boxed-final-answer scoring through `math_verify`, with the audit's confidence-box rule, should be pre-registered, because 2608.18379's answer-only result flips with the scoring rule. Every baseline should run through one harness, per-sample outputs should be released, and the existing analysis freeze should be extended so that the new contrasts are hashed before any trained model is evaluated.

## Conclusion

The field's question has shifted. It is no longer "can a trained reader beat voting?", which has been answered at equal K for this very model. It is now "does the win survive honest accounting, and does it come from reading?" The audit's numbers predict a specific, falsifiable pattern. A trained 1.7B reader can clear SC under generated-only accounting on level 5. It will struggle once prompt tokens or FLOPs count, unless it is gated or shrunk to 0.6B. That cost-rule-dependent verdict is the trained-aggregator counterpart of 2610.18786's finding that ensemble decompositions depend on the control chosen ([2610.18786](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18786v2)), and it is publishable whichever way it lands. The student's assets — per-problem matching, a frozen analysis, and 18 samples per dev problem — let it be measured rather than assumed.

The solver-side factorial turns the roadmap's largest risk, that solver-GRPO matches the reader, into a measured axis. It asks a question nobody has asked at 1.7B: is the diversity that helps a vote the same diversity that helps a reader? Paired with answer-swap controls, it also tests TTT-Consolidate's sharpest prediction, that RL turns in-context readers into voters. If that prediction holds, the thesis's two halves meet in one account. The audit showed that message content moves agents in both directions and cancels out at the vote. The reader study would show RL learning to lean on the vote instead of reading the content.
